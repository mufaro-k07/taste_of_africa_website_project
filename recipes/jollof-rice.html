Jollof Rice

West Africa

The story

Few dishes can start an argument across a whole continent the way jollof can. Nigerians, Ghanaians, Senegalese and Liberians all swear that theirs is the original and the best.

Many food historians trace the dish back to the Senegambia region, where a rice and tomato dish known as thieboudienne inspired the many versions we eat today. At weddings and birthdays, the jollof pot is the star, and a little scorched rice at the bottom, the party jollof smokiness, is a sign that it was cooked with confidence.

Cook it slowly, trust your nose, and be ready for opinions.

Method

Prepare
  1. Blend the tomatoes, red peppers, scotch bonnet and half the onions until smooth.
  2. Pour into a pot and boil until the water has reduced and the base is thick.
  3. Wash the rice until the water runs mostly clear.
Cook
  1. Heat the oil, fry the sliced onion until soft, then stir in the tomato paste and fry until it darkens.
  2. Add the reduced base and cook until the oil rises to the top.
  3. Add thyme, curry, bay leaves and stock cubes.
  4. Pour in the stock, add the rice, and stir once.
Serve
  1. Cover the pot tightly with foil and a lid, and cook on the lowest heat for about 30 minutes without stirring.
  2. Fold gently, taste for salt, and let it sit for 5 minutes.
  3. Serve with fried plantain and a cold drink.
Serves
4
Time
About 1 hour
Difficulty
Medium

Ingredients

Quantities shown for 4 servings.

  • 3 cups long grain parboiled rice
  • 4 large ripe tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers
  • 1 scotch bonnet pepper, to taste
  • 2 onions, one blended and one sliced
  • 3 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 0.5 cup vegetable oil
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1 teaspoon curry powder
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 stock cubes
  • 2 cups stock or water
  • Salt to taste
