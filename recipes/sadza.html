Sadza

Southern Africa

The story

In Zimbabwe, a meal without sadza hardly feels like a meal. Made from nothing more than white maize meal and water, it is stirred in a pot with a wooden spoon until it turns thick and smooth.

It is shaped into small balls with the fingers, pressed with a thumb to make a little scoop, and used to carry relish to the mouth, whether that is beef stew, wild greens, dried kapenta or a spoon of chakalaka. Sadza is eaten from the same plate by family and friends, which makes it as much about togetherness as it is about food.

The same maize porridge goes by other names across the region, like pap in South Africa and nsima in Malawi.

Method

Prepare
  1. Bring 2 cups of the water to a boil.
  2. Mix half a cup of the maize meal with the remaining cup of cold water into a smooth paste.
Cook
  1. Stir the paste into the boiling water and cook for about 5 minutes until it forms a thin porridge.
  2. Add the remaining maize meal in handfuls, pressing and stirring firmly with a wooden spoon after each addition until the sadza is thick and pulls away from the sides.
Serve
  1. Cover and let it steam on low heat for 5 minutes.
  2. Scoop it into a bowl, shape it into a smooth mound, and serve hot with a relish of your choice.
Serves
4
Time
About 30 minutes
Difficulty
Easy, but it takes a strong arm

Ingredients

Quantities shown for 4 servings.

  • 2 cups white maize meal
  • 3 cups water
  • A pinch of salt, optional
